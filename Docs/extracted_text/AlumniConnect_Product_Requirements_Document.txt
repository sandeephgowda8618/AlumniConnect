1. Product overview
AlumniConnect is a web-based Alumni–Student Mentorship and Mock Interview Platform. It connects 
students with alumni who have relevant skills, domain knowledge and industry experience. Students can 
discover mentors, request mentorship and book sessions. Alumni can manage their professional profiles and 
availability, respond to requests, conduct offline sessions and record outcomes. Administrators maintain 
platform reference data, manage users and review platform analytics.
Product principle
The product is more than a mentor directory. It manages the full lifecycle from discovery and request through 
allocation, scheduling, offline session outcomes and feedback, while demonstrating sound relational database design 
and specialised data stores.
1.1 Problem statement
Students may find it difficult to identify alumni whose technical skills, professional experience and career 
domain match their goals. Alumni need a consistent way to manage incoming requests, availability, sessions 
and student progress. Informal coordination makes it harder to track decisions, prevent scheduling conflicts 
and evaluate mentorship outcomes.
1.2 Product goals
Give students a structured way to discover suitable alumni mentors and understand why they are 
recommended.
Allow mentors to manage their expertise, availability, requests and session records.
Make mentorship requests, allocations and bookings traceable and protected by database integrity rules.
Capture session outcomes, two-way session feedback and student reviews of mentors.
Provide administrators with reference-data management and useful SQL-based operational reports.
Demonstrate DBMS concepts through MySQL transactions, Redis caching and Neo4j relationship-based 
recommendations.
1.3 Success indicators
Outcome
How success is demonstrated

Booking integrity

outcome.

DBMS demonstration

traversal.

3. Functional requirements
Requirement priority uses MUST for core-release behaviour and SHOULD for a capability that materially 
improves traceability or administration but may be staged if implementation time is constrained. Each 
acceptance statement is intended to be testable.
3.1 Identity, access and profiles
FR-01
Account registration and sign-in  [MUST]
The system shall allow a person to register and sign in using an account identity. A common User identity 
shall be associated with role-specific Student and/or Mentor profile information, so authentication data is 
not duplicated across profiles.
Acceptance: A registered user can authenticate; invalid credentials are rejected; the authenticated identity 
is consistently linked to the permitted profile(s).
FR-02
Role-based authorization  [MUST]
The system shall distinguish Student/Mentee, Alumni/Mentor and Administrator roles. Protected 
operations shall be available only to users whose roles and ownership permissions allow them.
Acceptance: A student cannot edit another user's profile or make mentor-only decisions; a mentor cannot 
access administrator controls; administrative operations require administrator authorization.
FR-03
Student profile management  [MUST]
A student shall be able to create, view and update their student profile, including relevant academic / 
department information, skills and mentorship interests.
Acceptance: The student can save changes and see the updated profile; skill and domain associations are 
represented without duplicating skill or domain master records.
FR-04
Mentor profile management  [MUST]
An alumni mentor shall be able to maintain a professional profile containing relevant experience, 
company, role, skills and mentorship-domain expertise.
Acceptance: The mentor can update professional information; changes are reflected in the authoritative 
MySQL record and eventually in the recommendation projection / caches.

FR-05
Skills and domain catalogue  [MUST]
The system shall maintain reusable Skill, Department, Company and Mentorship Domain reference

Acceptance: A student can add/remove skills and interests; the application presents the current saved

4. Functional requirements
4.1 Mentor discovery, recommendations and allocation
FR-08
Mentor directory and discovery  [MUST]
The student client shall provide a way to browse or search mentor profiles using relevant profile 
information such as skills, mentorship domains, company and professional experience. Private 
information shall not be exposed through the public-facing directory.
Acceptance: A student can retrieve matching mentor cards and view the mentor information permitted 
for discovery; private account data is not included in directory results.
FR-09
Graph-based mentor recommendations  [MUST]
The platform shall use Neo4j relationship traversal to identify potentially relevant mentors. 
Recommendation dimensions shall include skill similarity, domain relevance, relevant experience, career 
relevance, mentor quality and capacity. The relative weights shall be configurable rather than 
permanently hard-coded.
Acceptance: For a student profile, the platform returns candidate mentors and scores / matching 
dimensions; candidates who fail current MySQL eligibility or capacity checks are excluded from final 
results.
FR-10
Recommendation explanation and history  [SHOULD]
The system should explain why a mentor is recommended, for example shared skills, matching domain, 
relevant experience or prior mentoring outcomes. It should retain recommendation runs and candidate 
results so changes over time can be analysed.
Acceptance: A student can inspect the reasons for a recommendation; an administrator or authorised 
report can compare saved recommendation runs, scores and subsequent selection / mentorship outcomes.
FR-11
Mentorship request submission  [MUST]
A student shall be able to submit a mentorship request to a selected mentor. The request shall be tracked 
with a controlled lifecycle: Pending, Accepted, Rejected or Cancelled.
Acceptance: A request appears in the student's history and the mentor's pending requests; duplicate 
submissions are handled consistently; cancelled or rejected requests remain available for history and 
reporting.

FR-12
Mentor decision and allocation  [MUST]
A mentor shall be able to accept or reject a pending request. Before acceptance, the system shall verify that 
the request is still pending, the mentor can accept another allocation and no duplicate active allocation 
exists. An accepted request shall create a Mentor Allocation.
Acceptance: Accepting a request changes request status and creates at most one allocation in one MySQL 
transaction; a rejected request is retained in history; failed validation leaves no partial allocation.
4.2 Scheduling and booking
FR-13
Mentor availability management  [MUST]
A mentor shall be able to publish and maintain availability slots and preferred timings for mentorship or 
mock interview sessions.
Acceptance: A student can view slots that are currently eligible for booking; a slot that has already been 
claimed is not presented as available based solely on cached data.
FR-14
Atomic booking  [MUST]
A student shall be able to book an eligible mentor slot for a mentorship or mock interview. Before 
confirmation, the system shall validate slot availability, student-side overlapping appointments, mentor 
capacity, a valid request/allocation where required and duplicate submissions.
Acceptance: Under concurrent requests for the same slot, at most one booking succeeds; competing 
requests receive a clear unavailable/conflict response; database rollback leaves no partial booking.
FR-15
Booking cancellation and history  [MUST]
The system shall allow an authorised participant or administrator to cancel a booking according to the 
project's configured policy. Booking status changes and their history shall be retained.
Acceptance: A cancellation is persisted, the slot is recalculated from authoritative booking state and both 
participants are eligible for a cancellation notification; the record is not physically erased.

FR-17
Session feedback  [MUST]

Acceptance: Feedback cannot be submitted before the session has taken place; only the actual 
participants can submit it; the feedback is linked to the correct session.

FR-18
Mentor review and rating  [MUST]
The student shall be able to submit a mentor review after a completed session. A mentor review is distinct 
from general session feedback.
Acceptance: A review references a valid completed session; one student can submit at most one mentor 
review per session; review history is not silently overwritten.
5.2 Notifications and operations
FR-19
Event-driven notifications  [MUST]
The system shall generate notifications for key events: request submitted (mentor), request 
accepted/rejected (student), booking confirmed/cancelled (both participants), session reminder (both 
participants) and feedback submitted (relevant participant).
Acceptance: Each committed event creates the corresponding notification intent; an operation that rolls 
back does not result in a delivered notification; notification processing can be retried safely.
FR-20
Transactional outbox processing  [MUST]
The platform shall record notification and projection events in a MySQL transactional outbox within the 
same business transaction. A background worker shall process committed outbox events to deliver 
notifications, update Neo4j projections and invalidate relevant Redis cache entries.
Acceptance: If the business transaction rolls back, no outbox event is committed; if a worker retries an 
event, duplicate side effects are prevented or safely handled.
FR-21
Administration of users and reference data  [MUST]
Administrators shall be able to manage users and the reference data required by the platform, including 
departments, skills and mentorship domains.
Acceptance: An administrator can perform permitted management actions; an unauthorised user cannot 
invoke them; changes are auditable.
FR-22
Operational analytics and reporting  [MUST]
The administrator shall be able to access SQL-backed reports for platform oversight. Useful reports include 
request-to-allocation-to-session funnel, mentor ratings, completed sessions, mentors by domain and 
students without an allocation.
Acceptance: Reports are derived from authoritative data and return consistent counts / aggregates for the 
selected criteria; private fields are excluded unless required by an authorised administrative task.
FR-23
Redis cache management  [MUST]
The system shall use Redis for rebuildable performance data such as frequently viewed mentor profiles, 
domain/skill reference data and recent recommendation results. It may also store short-lived login 
challenges, rate-limit counters and idempotency records. Cache-aside, TTL and invalidation shall be used 
where appropriate.
Acceptance: The system demonstrates cache hit, miss and expiry; profile changes invalidate relevant 
cache entries; a cache hit never bypasses MySQL authorization or booking validation.

6. Business rules and data requirements
6.1 Mandatory business rules
Rule ID
Business rule
BR-01
A user has one common authentication identity. Student and Mentor information is held in separate role-specific profile 
concepts.
BR-02
A student can have multiple skills; a mentor can have multiple skills. These many-to-many associations must be represented 
explicitly.
BR-03
A mentorship request has one controlled status: Pending, Accepted, Rejected or Cancelled. Rejected and cancelled requests 
remain in history.
BR-04
A request may result in at most one allocation. Acceptance validates current request state, mentor capacity and duplicate 
active allocations.
BR-05
A booking is a planned appointment; a Mentorship Session is the actual meeting and its outcome. These are separate concepts.
BR-06
A specific mentor availability slot can be booked at most once. Student-side overlapping appointments must also be prevented.
BR-07
MySQL is authoritative for availability, bookings, allocation, session status, ratings and feedback. Redis must not make the 
final booking decision.
BR-08
Neo4j recommendations are candidate suggestions. Current eligibility and capacity are checked against MySQL before action.
BR-09
Neo4j and Redis are synchronised from committed MySQL events. Neo4j may be eventually consistent; MySQL wins if records 
disagree.
BR-10
Session feedback is available only after the session has taken place and only to actual participants.
BR-11
A student may submit at most one mentor review per completed session. Review history must be preserved.
BR-12
Notification intents are committed through the outbox in the same transaction as the business change that generated them.

6.4 Data authority and synchronisation
MySQL is the system of record for identity, profiles, skills, requests, allocations, availability, bookings, 
sessions, ratings, feedback, notifications and audit records.
Redis stores temporary or rebuildable data for speed. Use TTL and explicit invalidation for profile and 
recommendation caches. Never trust it as the authority for booking availability.
Neo4j stores a graph representation of student, mentor, skill, domain, company and department 
relationships. It is a recommendation projection, not the authoritative store for profile or booking data.
On a relevant profile or relationship change: commit MySQL data and an outbox event together; process 
the event asynchronously; update Neo4j; invalidate affected Redis keys.
7. Non-functional requirements
Interpretation of this section
The project blueprint specifies architecture and business safeguards but does not set numeric service-level targets. The 
measurable thresholds below are proposed initial acceptance targets for this academic project. They should be 
confirmed against the team's deployment environment and test data before final sign-off.
NFR-01
Security and authorization  [MUST]
All protected operations shall enforce authorization on the backend, not only by hiding interface controls. 
Passwords shall be stored using secure one-way salted hashing; secrets shall not be committed to source 
control.
Acceptance: Role and ownership tests cover student, mentor and administrator actions; unauthorised API 
calls are rejected; plaintext passwords and secrets are not present in the database or logs.
NFR-02
Privacy and data exposure  [MUST]
The platform shall expose only the profile attributes required for mentor discovery to users who are 
authorised to view them. Administrative, authentication and private account data shall not be included in 
public directory responses.
Acceptance: A directory/API response test confirms that private fields are omitted; access to private 
records is restricted to the owner or authorised administrator.
NFR-03
Transactional integrity  [MUST]
Allocation, booking, cancellation and event creation shall preserve consistency through database 
transactions and integrity constraints. Concurrent booking requests shall not create duplicate slot 
reservations or overlapping student appointments.
Acceptance: Automated concurrent tests show one successful reservation per slot; injected failures roll 
back all related changes; uniqueness and foreign-key constraints reject invalid references.

NFR-04
Cross-database consistency  [MUST]
MySQL shall remain authoritative. Redis and Neo4j updates shall be driven from committed outbox events 
and be recoverable through retries/rebuilds. Stale cached or graph data shall not override current MySQL 
validation.
Acceptance: A committed profile change eventually updates the graph projection and invalidates related 
cache entries; a rolled-back change is not projected; booking remains correct if Redis or Neo4j is stale or 
unavailable.
NFR-05
Performance - proposed baseline  [SHOULD]
For an initial test profile of up to 50 concurrent users, common read/API operations should complete 
within 2.5 seconds at the 95th percentile; mentor recommendations within 4 seconds; booking 
confirmation within 3 seconds, excluding external network delays.
Acceptance: The team executes a repeatable load test and records p50/p95 latency, throughput and error 
rate; results are documented with dataset size and environment. These are project targets, not guarantees 
from the blueprint.
NFR-06
Scalability and modularity  [SHOULD]
The backend shall be organised as a modular monolith with clearly separated business modules and a 
shared primary relational database. Modules should be separable in code without requiring independent 
microservice deployment.
Acceptance: Identity, profile, matching, request, booking, session, feedback and notification logic have 
clear module boundaries and can be tested independently.
8. Non-functional requirements
NFR-07
Reliability and graceful failure  [MUST]
A failed database operation shall not leave partial business state. Temporary failures in notification 
delivery or graph/cache projection shall be retryable without losing the committed business transaction.
Acceptance: Failure injection tests show transaction rollback and safe worker retry; users receive a clear 
result when an operation cannot be completed.
NFR-08
Auditability and history  [MUST]
Important state changes shall be traceable through audit/history data, including request decisions, 
allocation, booking changes, session outcomes and administrative changes. Historical records shall not be 
silently erased or overwritten.
Acceptance: An authorised reviewer can trace key lifecycle changes to actor, time and target record; 
review and request history remains available after status changes.
NFR-09
Usability and responsive layout  [SHOULD]
Student, mentor and administrator workflows shall use role-appropriate navigation and clear 
validation/error messages. The web interface should remain usable on common desktop, tablet and mobile 
viewport sizes.
Acceptance: Representative workflows can be completed without relying on hidden or role-inappropriate 
controls; key pages render without horizontal overflow at the agreed viewport test sizes.
NFR-10
Accessibility  [SHOULD]
Forms, navigation, status indicators and interactive controls should support keyboard use, visible focus 
and meaningful labels. Colour shall not be the only indicator of status.
Acceptance: A manual accessibility review verifies keyboard access, labels, focus visibility and readable 
contrast for the main workflows.

8.1 Operational constraints
The primary relational database is MySQL 8.x with InnoDB; it is the final authority for transactional 
business rules.
Redis is used for caching and short-lived state. A cache outage must not make incorrect bookings possible.
Neo4j is used for relationship-based discovery. MySQL validates capacity, identity and final eligibility.
The backend is a modular monolith with one logical API boundary; independent microservice 
deployments are not required.
Notification delivery and cross-database projection are asynchronous worker responsibilities backed by a 
transactional outbox.

AlumniConnect application evidence

10.1 Suggested end-to-end acceptance demonstration
1.
A student signs in, updates skills and domain interests, and discovers mentors with explainable 
recommendation reasons.
2.
The student submits a request. The mentor accepts it. The system creates one allocation and triggers a 
notification through the outbox.
3.
The mentor publishes a slot. Two test clients attempt to book it concurrently; only one booking is 
confirmed.
4.
The offline session is recorded as completed with topics, skills assessed, observations and next steps.
5.
The participants submit structured feedback and the student submits one mentor review for the 
completed session.
6.
The administrator views a request-to-allocation-to-session report and a mentor-rating report.
7.
The team demonstrates Redis cache hit/miss/expiry and Neo4j recommendation traversal, then explains 
why MySQL remains authoritative.
